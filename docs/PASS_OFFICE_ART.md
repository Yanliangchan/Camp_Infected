# Pass office art review

The first room is an enclosed checkpoint and pass office rather than the previous open camp diorama. The user approved its look and detail, then requested more space for three players. Its footprint is now 20 × 16 metres (320 m²), expanded from 14 × 10 (140 m²). Furniture and tile scale remain unchanged. The entry is widened to 3.6 metres; added perimeter storage leaves the central combat floor open. Three soldier figures plus two enemy figures demonstrate spacing in the static preview. Combat, navigation and online progression remain disconnected pending their implementation review.

The palette is an artistic interpretation of an older Singapore institutional interior: off-white painted plaster, muted olive lower walls and steel, grey tile, charcoal frames and modest laminate furniture. It is not a verified universal SAF colour specification or a replica of any named camp. Lockdown lighting supplies the dungeon atmosphere; Inspection light lets the reviewer judge the underlying colours.

The room provides visitor registration and waiting areas, a glazed service counter, staff workstation and a controlled onward door. Details include document slot, trays, logbook, passes, CCTV monitor, keyboard/mouse/cables, telephone, radio charger, filing drawers, linked chairs, fan, louvres, conduit, sockets, key cabinet, first-aid box, extinguisher, drainage grate, floor wear and emergency signage. Furniture is original geometry; public reference photos are not bundled as game assets. The public registration area has no open weapon rack.

Camera controls: Room overview, Game camera and Pass counter. The last two are close-up review views; they are not evidence of implemented gameplay. Static opaque fixtures sharing materials are batched for rendering.

## Guard rest wing expansion
The first-area visual study now covers 40 x 16 metres (640 square metres), twice the previous floor area. The original pass counter and open squad combat floor retain their detail and scale. The public entrance adds a walk-through metal detector, bag-only X-ray conveyor, curtains, trays and operator workstation. Generic public screening equipment informs the visual design; this is not an exact SAF equipment model.

An open 3.3 metre double doorway leads through the shared wall to a guard rest wing. It includes two worn couches, a low table and TV; a dining table with four chairs and kitchenette; four two-tier bunks with blankets, pillows and ladders; seven lockers; and three toilet cubicles with basins and mirrors. Low walls are cutaways for art inspection. The central passage and original combat floor stay spacious. New camera buttons inspect Screening, Guard rest, Bunks and Toilets.

This is still a visual preview for user review. Combat, collisions and room progression have not been connected to this expanded layout.

## Door clearance and close-up bunk revision
Oversized guard rest doors are replaced with 1.2 metre single openings and 2.2 metre door height. Open leaves park flat against the wall. Toilet and bunk entrances use the same scale. The bunk entrance is centered at x24, z5.5 and opens onto the clear central aisle. Storage near the shared guard-room approach has moved down the wall; the locker beside the controlled rear exit has moved to x8, z-4.5.

Beds now follow the user's supplied SAF bunk photograph: silver rounded tube frames, full ladders, fasteners and slats, white sheets/pillows, one olive towel, personal kit and grey vented lockers with label cards. One locker exposes shelving, hanging kit and boots. Bed rows are at z2.15 and z9.73 with the front row reversed so ladders face the center. Heads sit close to their walls. The central space between ladder edges extends approximately from z3.23 to z8.65. Loose personal items remain within bed footprints.

Staff desks, filing cabinets, locker storage, noticeboard, lounge TV/cabinet, kitchenette, bathroom mirrors and basins now align to the actual inner wall faces. Mounted items have backing or bracket details. The washroom retains wall backing for mirrors. Ceiling-cutaway fixtures over the bunk floor have been removed in favor of visible wall-aligned lights and ambient fills.

Bed detail and Doorway review cameras plus an optional Show routes overlay allow visual inspection at closer scales. Routes are a presentation overlay, not active pathfinding/collision gameplay.
